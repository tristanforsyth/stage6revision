HSC Exam Guide

What is in the Society & Culture written examination, where your course areas appear, and how to manage the two hours.

The written paper at a glance

60 marks

The written examination is worth 60 marks. The Personal Interest Project is worth the other 40 marks of the HSC assessment.

2 hours

You have 2 hours writing time, plus 5 minutes reading time.

2 sections

Section I examines the Core. Section II examines your two Depth Studies.

Structure based on the current NESA Society & Culture HSC examination specifications.

Section II — Depth Studies

NESA provides one question for each Depth Study. Each question has two unrelated parts: a 5-mark part and a 15-mark part. You answer the questions for two Depth Studies.

NESA states that the expected length for each complete Depth Study response is around six pages, approximately 800 words. Treat this as an indication of expected scope rather than a target that replaces answering the question well.

A practical two-hour plan

The exam is 60 marks in 120 minutes, so a useful starting point is roughly 2 minutes per mark. The timings below are a revision strategy, not a NESA rule.

Reading time — 5 min

Read the paper carefully. Identify command words, required course areas and any choices. Begin thinking about evidence for the longer responses.

Section I — about 40 min

20 marks × roughly 2 minutes per mark. Keep short answers proportionate to their mark value.

Popular Culture — about 40 min

Complete both the 5-mark and 15-mark parts. Spend most of this time on the 15-marker.

Beliefs & Ideologies — about 40 min

Complete both parts. Keep enough time to finish and make your judgement/argument clear.

Simple checkpoint: after roughly 40 minutes of writing you should be moving out of Section I; after roughly 80 minutes you should be moving to your final Depth Study.

What to do in reading time

  • Read every part of the question — not just the topic.
  • Identify the command word: explain, analyse, assess, evaluate, discuss, etc.
  • Circle mentally the course concepts the question is really testing.
  • For Section II, identify 2–4 strong pieces of case-study evidence you could use.
  • Decide the basic argument or judgement for the 15-mark questions before writing.
