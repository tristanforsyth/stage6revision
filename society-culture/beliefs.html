Beliefs & Ideologies

Revise the Society & Culture concepts through Islam as the class case study.

Society & Culture focus: this is not a Religious Studies treatment of Islam. Focus on how belief systems and ideologies influence individuals, groups and societies.
IslamWorldviewIdentityCommunityAuthoritySecularisation
1. Foundations: belief systems, ideologies and worldviews

Belief systems and ideologies provide frameworks through which people understand the world. They can provide meaning, purpose, certainty, values, belonging and explanations for social experience, while also influencing behaviour, decision-making and attitudes towards change.

A worldview shapes how individuals and groups interpret social issues and contemporary events. Beliefs are also influenced by socialisation, culture, family, education, media and personal experience. The unit uses Islam as a Society & Culture case study rather than as a study of religious doctrine.

2. Identity and belonging

Belief systems can shape both personal and collective identity. Identity is influenced by values, relationships, culture, religion, ethnicity, gender, education, peer groups and life experiences, and individuals may hold multiple identities at the same time.

Belonging develops through shared beliefs, values, practices and participation. In Islam, the Ummah is useful evidence for collective identity because it connects adherents to a wider Muslim community while still allowing for diversity in individual and cultural expression.

Revision focus: Explain the relationship: belief → values/behaviour → identity → belonging/participation.
3. Community and participation

Belief systems can create and maintain communities by establishing shared values, expectations, language and practices. Participation can strengthen social connections, reinforce collective identity and transmit cultural knowledge between generations.

For Islam, consider the Ummah, mosques, religious gatherings, charity and youth/community organisations. These can be analysed at micro, meso and macro levels to show how individuals, communities and wider institutions interact.

4. Rituals, symbols, customs and language

Beliefs are expressed and reinforced through rituals, symbols, customs, traditions and language. These practices communicate shared meanings, strengthen identity and belonging, and help transmit beliefs and values across generations.

Islamic examples include Salah, Ramadan, Hajj and Zakat as ritual practices; mosques, Arabic calligraphy and the Qur'an as important symbolic expressions; and customs associated with Ramadan, Eid, family gatherings and community life.

These forms of expression are also useful for continuity and change because traditions may be maintained, adapted or reinterpreted in changing social and cultural contexts.

5. Authority, power and institutions

Power is the capacity to influence people or decisions, while authority is power regarded as legitimate or accepted. Belief systems can create forms of authority through institutions, traditions, sacred texts and recognised leadership.

Institutions help maintain continuity, social order and community stability by transmitting values and expectations. In the Islam case study, the Qur'an, religious leaders, mosques, traditions and community expectations can be examined as different sources or expressions of authority.

Exam focus: Do not simply list sources of authority. Explain how they influence adherents, participation, behaviour or continuity.
6. Continuity and change

Belief systems can act as forces for both continuity and change. Shared values, rituals, traditions and institutions can preserve identity and social cohesion, while changing social conditions may lead to adaptation, reinterpretation, reform or dissent.

Influences on change include technology, globalisation, migration, generational change and changing social values. A strong response recognises that continuity and change can occur simultaneously: core beliefs may remain while the ways they are expressed or practised adapt to new circumstances.

7. Secularisation, acceptance and rejection

Secularisation refers to changes in the social influence, participation or authority of organised religion within society. It can contribute to changing patterns of belief, the growth of non-religious worldviews and different attitudes towards religious institutions.

Acceptance or rejection of a belief system can also be influenced by representation, cultural diversity, migration, globalisation, social values and personal experience. When using Islam, distinguish between the diversity of adherents and communities rather than presenting the belief system as uniform.

8. Contemporary society and future directions

Contemporary belief systems operate within increasingly diverse, globalised and technologically connected societies. Communication technologies can alter how beliefs are expressed, shared and discussed, while migration and multiculturalism can create new forms of community and identity.

For future directions, use current trends rather than guessing. Consider the interaction between secularisation, diversity, technology, globalisation, changing values and the continuing importance of identity, belonging and community.
