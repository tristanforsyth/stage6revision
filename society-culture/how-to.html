How to Answer

Use the question, command word, concepts and evidence to control your response.

This section turns NESA marking feedback into a practical revision routine. It is not a magic essay formula: the question, mark value and command word determine what the response needs.

What NESA repeatedly rewards

  • Understand the key word and every part of the question.
  • Use relevant Society & Culture terminology and concepts.
  • Use specific examples, information and statistics where relevant.
  • Show depth and range of course knowledge rather than listing everything you know.
  • Make responses logical and cohesive.
  • For extended responses, plan first and sustain the argument or judgement throughout.
NESA marking guidelines describe the quality required for marks. Their sample answers are guides to expected scope, not complete model answers.

1. Decode the question

Explain

Show how or why. Make the relationship clear. Do not stop at a definition or example.

Analyse

Break the issue into relevant parts and explain the relationships between them.

Assess

Make a judgement about value, impact, importance or extent, supported by evidence.

Evaluate

Make and sustain a judgement using criteria/evidence. Avoid leaving the judgement until the conclusion.

Discuss

Present relevant issues or perspectives and reach a supported position where appropriate.

2. Let the marks tell you the depth

MarksWhat to aim for
3–4Direct answer; accurate concept; one or more relevant pieces of evidence; explain the link. Be concise.
5–8Developed explanation/analysis; several linked ideas or examples; explicit course concepts; address every part of the question.
9+Sustained argument or judgement; depth and range; specific case-study evidence; concepts integrated throughout; logical structure.
15Plan first. Establish a clear line of argument, develop it with several strong examples, integrate concepts and return to the question throughout.

Use the mark value as a guide to depth, not as a rigid paragraph-count formula.

3. Build the response

Short response: Answer the question immediately → use the relevant concept → give specific evidence → explain how the evidence proves the point → link back to the wording of the question.

Extended response: Decide your argument/judgement → select 2–4 strongest lines of evidence → attach relevant Society & Culture concepts → sequence them logically → keep answering the exact question in every section.

4. Use concepts analytically

Weak: “Globalisation has affected South Korea.”

Stronger: “Globalisation has accelerated cultural change in South Korea through increased flows of media, technology and consumer culture; however, the adaptation of global influences alongside Korean traditions demonstrates that change does not automatically remove cultural continuity.”

The second version does something with the concept: it explains a relationship and makes a judgement about continuity and change.

5. Use your case studies as evidence

South Korea

Do not write a general country history. Select evidence that proves the claim about modernisation, westernisation, globalisation, theory, resistance, continuity/change or future directions.

Teen Film

Name specific films, industries, audiences, technologies and patterns. Explain what they show about development, consumption, control, gender, identity or change.

Islam

Use specific beliefs, rituals, symbols, institutions and contemporary examples. Connect them to meaning, identity, belonging, authority, secularisation or continuity/change.

6. Common problems to avoid

  • Writing everything you remember instead of answering the question.
  • Defining a concept but never applying it.
  • Giving an example without explaining what it proves.
  • Using generic evidence when the question requires a specific case study.
  • Ignoring one part of a multi-part question.
  • Making a judgement only in the final sentence of an assess/evaluate response.
  • Describing change without considering rate, direction, scale, resistance or different levels of society when relevant.
