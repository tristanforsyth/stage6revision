Continuity & Change

1. Nature of continuity and change

Continuity refers to aspects of society and culture that remain relatively stable over time, while change involves alteration in social or cultural patterns. Both can occur at the same time: a society may adopt new technologies or values while maintaining traditions, expectations or institutions.

When examining change, consider its rate, direction and scale, the forces encouraging or resisting it, and whether the effects are evolutionary or transformative. Also consider who benefits, who may be disadvantaged, and whether change creates greater stability, inequality or tension.

Revision focus: What changed? What remained? Why? At what level of society? Who benefited or resisted?
2. Micro / Meso / Macro

Use micro, meso and macro as levels of analysis rather than as separate topics. Micro focuses on individuals and close relationships; meso on groups, communities and organisations; macro on wider institutions, structures and society-wide patterns.

Change can move in more than one direction. Individual attitudes can influence groups and institutions, while government policy, economic change or technology can reshape everyday behaviour. In South Korea, examples such as educational expectations, changing gender roles, democratic participation, technological change and cultural identity can be examined across all three levels.

Exam tip: Moving between levels is a strong way to show the complexity of social change.
3. Modernisation, Westernisation and globalisation

Modernisation involves processes such as industrialisation, urbanisation, economic development, technological advancement and educational expansion. Westernisation is the adoption or influence of Western cultural practices, values, institutions and lifestyles. Globalisation increases the movement of people, ideas, goods, technologies and culture across national boundaries.

These processes can overlap, but they are not the same. South Korea is useful because it demonstrates rapid modernisation while also showing Western influence, global cultural exchange, adaptation and continuing Korean traditions.

South Korea evidence: advanced technology and digital infrastructure; economic growth and educational achievement; Western influence on food, fashion, media and social attitudes; K-pop, K-drama and film as global cultural exports; cultural hybridisation where local and global influences combine.
4. Theories of social change

Social theories are frameworks for explaining the causes, processes and directions of continuity and change. You need to understand the basic assumptions of each theory and be able to apply them to South Korean evidence.

  • Evolutionary Theory: change occurs gradually over time, often through increasing complexity or development. Useful for technological development, education and changing social values.
  • Functionalist Theory: institutions adapt in response to social needs in order to maintain stability and social order. Useful for educational reform, economic development and demographic change.
  • Conflict Theory: change results from inequality, competition, power struggles and competing interests. Useful for protest, labour movements, gender equality movements and democratic reform.
  • Interactionist Theory: change can emerge through everyday interactions, communication and changing meanings. Useful for changing attitudes towards identity, family, gender roles and cultural expectations.
Exam focus: Do not just define a theory. Apply it to evidence and judge how well it explains the change.
5. Responses to change: adaptation, resistance and continuity

Social and cultural change does not automatically replace what came before. Individuals and groups may accept, adapt to or resist change depending on values, power, identity and perceived benefits or disadvantages.

South Korea provides examples of cultural resistance where traditional beliefs or practices continue, adaptation where traditions are modified for contemporary circumstances, and cultural hybridisation where Korean and global influences combine. This is why continuity and change should often be analysed together rather than as opposites.

6. South Korea evidence bank

Candlelight Revolution

The 2016–17 protests are useful for power, authority, democratic participation, collective action, resistance and social change. They can also be used with Conflict Theory and micro–meso–macro analysis.

Technology & Modernisation

Advanced technology industries, digital infrastructure, high-speed internet and smart technologies demonstrate rapid modernisation and the role of technology in accelerating the rate and direction of change.

Identity, Family & Gender

Changing gender roles, family structures, educational expectations and increasing individualism can be compared with continuing social expectations and cultural values.

Global & Cultural Influence

K-pop, K-drama, film, fashion, food and consumer culture show South Korea as both a recipient and exporter of cultural influence. These examples are useful for globalisation, Westernisation, adaptation and hybridisation.

7. Future directions

Future directions should be based on current evidence and identifiable trends, not unsupported prediction. Consider whether existing patterns are likely to continue, accelerate, be resisted or produce new forms of adaptation.

Useful areas include demographic change, technology, education, changing gender roles, cultural identity and global influence. A strong prediction explains the trend, its likely impact and why continuity or change is the most reasonable outcome.
